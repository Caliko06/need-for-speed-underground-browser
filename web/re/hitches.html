Hitches in `runs/w_L1/log.txt`: what `spike:` and `SMOOTHJOB` are

Rendering & Shaders 28 verified Speed.exe addresses Speed.exe (2003, x86)
Updated 2026-11-25 View raw Markdown (.md) Source on GitHub

Log: runs/w_L1/log.txt (37,033 lines). Nothing was built or run; every number below comes from reading that log and pc/Speed.exe (objdump, image base 0x400000) plus the host sources. Markers: F = read in the log or in the code, I = inference from that evidence, NV = not verified (no run, no counter).

1. What the two line types are

spike: (5,671 lines) — host/d3d9.c:817-841, spike_log(), called at Present. It logs only when all of these hold (F, source): the frame is longer than 25,000 us Present-to-Present, the game state [0x77a920] == 6 (race), the race manager [0x7361f0] is set, and [race_manager] != 1. So the probe sees race frames only; menus and loads are invisible to it. The payload is not a stack sample: host/win32.c:559-566 clk_site() counts, per frame and on the main thread only, every clock read (QueryPerformanceCounter, GetTickCount, timeGetTime) keyed by the return address of the game's call *0x69717c; the line prints the 3 hottest sites with their counts.

  • All 5,671 spikes have reads 0 (0 KB, 0.0 ms) (F). No spike in this run is disk/file streaming.
  • The three sites are almost always the same family: 0x403a7a in 5,670 of 5,671 (2 reads, the per-frame timer),

0x474c24/0x474aa9 in 5,053, 0x585bf7 in 353, 0x477de0/0x477511 in 263 (F).

SMOOTHJOB (425 lines) — host/d3d9.c:2464, only with RN_FREELOG set. It is not a hitch: it is the main thread picking up one finished async car-smoothing job (host/carsmooth.c, worker thread) and copying its vertices into the cache slot. 425 jobs for the whole race, cumulative worker CPU 45.9 ms = 0.11 ms per job (F). Ignore it as a cause; it only tells you how many car meshes were smoothed on first sight.

2. Size of the problem

  • 5,671 spikes over 35,634 race frames (frames 3060 to 38693) = 15.9 % of race frames over 25 ms (F).
  • 335 s of the run sit inside spike frames (F, sum of the logged ms).
  • gpu: windows (100 frames each, 389 of them) give the whole-run frame time: mean 23.0 ms (43 fps), median

17.4, p10 16.7, p90 30.2 (F). CPU raster thread: 0.8 % of the wall (F) — the raster backend is idle, this is not GPU.

  • Not isolated stutters: 75 % of spikes are followed by another spike (base rate 15.9 %), 87 % of spikes live in

runs of 2+, longest run 248 consecutive frames, and the 116 runs of >= 10 frames hold 54 % of all spike frames (mean 26 frames, mean 78.5 ms per frame) (F). The bad stretches are self-sustaining.

4. Where the time is: two named loops (disassembly of pc/Speed.exe)

The substep loop, 0x474a90 (the physics step; docs/re/race_ai.md:51). At 0x474be4: flds 0x8(%ebp) / fdivs 0x6fff10 (= 0.0084 s) / call 0x674898 / incl %eax -> n = (int)(dt / 8.4 ms) + 1; then 0x474c10..0x474c4b loops n times, each pass reading QPC (the site logged as 0x474c24), calling the Euler step 0x46aa40 and one more QPC read (F, disasm at 0x474be4-0x474c4b).

The dt that reaches that division is clamped: the limiter's clock at 0x585c6a loads [0x6f0894] = 3.0 (a max of 3 frame targets) and 0x585c82/0x585ca3 multiply it by 0x6cc7d0 = 0.02 or 0x6cc7b0 = 0.016667 and keep the smaller of dt and that product, i.e. dt <= 60 ms or 50 ms (F for the constants and the min pattern, I for which branch). That is exactly what the log shows: over the bad stretches 0x474c24 reads / 0x474aa9 step calls = 6.0 (168/28, 120/20, 188/32, 212/37), i.e. 6 substeps of 8.4 ms = 50 ms, the clamp, no matter whether the frame took 685 ms or 143 ms (F). So the amplifier is real but saturates at ~6 steps: a frame longer than 50 ms cannot shorten itself, and each of those 6 steps costs 4-7 ms per car in the worst frames instead of the ~0.1 ms it needs at 60 fps (I from the same counts).

The limiter spin, 0x585bf7 (F, host/win32.c:567-573 comment + disasm: call *0x69717c at 0x585bf5, return 0x585bf7, loop until the wait expires). 353 spikes have 100 % of their clock reads there, median 32,939 reads in a median 27.9 ms frame: that whole interval is the game spinning on its own 60 Hz limiter, not work. Their share falls to 0 % above 250 ms, as expected (a frame that is really late does not wait).

0x477de0 (263 spikes) is a QPC read inside the per-car frame update 0x4774a0; its count is cars x substeps (x12 = 12 car updates = one pass over ~12 nearby cars, F for the site; I for the decomposition). These are the worst: median 58.7 ms, mean 87.9 ms, and 14-15 % of the 250 ms+ buckets.

The (1846,-727) spot. That exact coordinate appears in this repo only as an AIHIT wall hit in other runs: runs/w_a/log.txt:17230 (f12390), runs/w_b/log.txt:20286 (f14299), runs/w_d/log.txt:2555 (f5237), all at 1846,-727,53 — a wall that a rival hits deterministically (F). In w_L1:

  • the player never gets closer than 102 m to it (minimum at frame 2880, spawn area 1861,-626) (F);
  • 0 spikes within 200 m, 391 spikes (6.9 %) in the 200-400 m band, at a rate of 0.096, i.e. below the 0.159

baseline; 93 % of spikes happen 400 m+ away (F);

  • the only traffic cars logged near it are carpos 40.55 20 1790.5 -729.5 and 44.61 20 1782.7 -709.5 (F).

So in w_L1 that wall is not a hitch hotspot (F). In w_a and w_b the +-120 frames around their 1846,-727 hit are 241/241 spiking frames, but those two runs spike 19,416 and 17,911 times overall — they are bad everywhere, so the coincidence says nothing about the spot (F for the counts, I for the reading). If the OWNER saw a hitch there, it is from w_a/w_b, not from L1, and it needs its own log with spike: positions (NV).

7. Ranked guess at the causes

  1. CPU overrun inside the physics substep loop, self-sustaining. F: 88.6 % of spikes are dominated by the

0x474c24 site, which lives in a loop whose count is (int)(dt/8.4 ms)+1, dt clamped at 50-60 ms (so ~6 steps); 75 % of spikes follow a spike and 54 % of spike frames sit in 116 stretches of >= 10 frames. I: the game is behind, runs its clamped 6 substeps, is still behind, and the stretch only ends when the car/work mix changes.

  1. Per-car cost blow-up (contact/solver), not per-car count. F: inside the worst stretches the site count is

constant (x120, x168, x188) while ms varies 5-10x — 6 substeps x 20-32 cars every frame, yet 4-7 ms per car-step against ~0.1 ms at 60 fps; AIHIT co-occurs in 19 % of 25-30 ms spikes rising to 54 % of 500 ms+, and the worst five all sit in stretches with rival-vs-rival or wall contact at 4-15 m. I: some per-car contact/solver path gets expensive when cars pile up; the clock sites cannot see inside a single step, so this is the open part.

  1. The 60 Hz limiter inflates 6.2 % of "spikes". F: 353 frames are 100 % 0x585bf7 spin, ~33k QPC reads, median

27.9 ms — the interval is the wait, not work. Do not chase these: they are not hitches, they are the limiter landing late (or its wait target being wrong: I).

  1. Nothing from I/O, loads, refills or the GPU. F: 0 of 5,671 spikes read a file; REFILLWHY is at baseline;

flow/load lines are 1-3 % of spikes; the raster thread uses 0.8 % of the wall time.

What would settle #1 vs #2 (NV, needs a run): a counter of dt, substep count and the per-step now_us() inside 0x474a90 (or a host timer around 0x46aa40) printed only when a frame passes 25 ms — that separates "more steps" from "slower steps" without guessing. A second, cheaper probe: log [0x6f0738] (the step index written at 0x474c19) with the spike line.

No patch is proposed: the evidence names two loops but not a defect, and any change to 8.4 ms, the substep count or the limiter would change physics timing with nothing measured behind it. No tools/recomp/cand/memo.py was written.
